## Receipts

### R1 — Texas holds $280 million in Israel bonds
- **Feb 12, 2026:** Acting Comptroller Kelly Hancock doubled the state's Israel bond holding
  from ~$140M to **$280M** — "the largest one-time investment in Israel bonds in Texas
  history." Texas went from the 6th- to the **2nd-largest US state investor**.
  Hancock, verbatim: *"Texas proudly stands with Israel. This expanded investment reinforces
  our long-standing relationship and shared commitment to faith, freedom and economic
  opportunity."*
  → https://comptroller.texas.gov/about/media-center/news/20260212-acting-comptroller-kelly-hancock-doubles-texas-investment-in-israel-bonds-marking-largest-purchase-in-state-history-1770753243996
- **Nov 2, 2023:** Comptroller Glenn Hegar bought **$45M**, taking the total to ~$140M.
  Fixed rate **5.4%**, **3-year** maturity. Hegar, verbatim: *"I am pleased to be able to
  continue supporting Israel... Israel is also a reliable counterparty, and their perfect
  record of interest and principal payments make this a solid investment for Texas."*
  → https://comptroller.texas.gov/about/media-center/news/20231102-texas-comptroller-glenn-hegar-announces-45-million-purchase-in-israel-bonds-1698872583849
- Texas has bought Israel bonds yearly **since 1994**.

**Honesty notes — do not overstate:**
- These are **bonds**: loans that pay Texas interest, not gifts. The accurate line is "Texas
  parks $280M of state money in Israeli government debt," NOT "Texas gives Israel $280M."
- **Neither release discloses which fund** the money comes from. The purchases are made by the
  **Comptroller** (Hancock/Hegar), not by Abbott.
- **UNSUPPORTED:** that the *Permanent School Fund* holds Israel bonds. Could not verify.
  **The arch must not claim school money buys Israel bonds.**
- But see **R8** — the PSF *is* named in the anti-BDS divestment statute by name. That is the
  verified schools↔Israel link, and it is a different (and better) claim.

### R8 — the Permanent School Fund is bound by the Israel-boycott law BY NAME
- **Gov't Code § 808.001(6)**, verbatim, defining who the divestment law covers:
  > "State governmental entity" means: (A) the Employees Retirement System of Texas...
  > (B) the Teacher Retirement System of Texas; (C) the Texas Municipal Retirement System;
  > (D) the Texas County and District Retirement System; (E) the Texas Emergency Services
  > Retirement System; and **(F) the permanent school fund.**
- Chapter 808 was created by **HB 89 (2017)** — the same Abbott-signed bill as the contractor
  certification in R2. § 808.051: the Comptroller "shall prepare and maintain... a list of all
  companies that boycott Israel." § 808.054: listed companies must be divested **50% by day
  180, 100% by day 360**.
- **The current list (Q4 2025) has 10 companies**, including **Ben & Jerry's Homemade, Inc.**
  and **Unilever PLC** (added Sept 2021, never removed), Co-operative Group, DNB Bank ASA, KLP.
  **Airbnb** was listed March 1 2019 and gone by May 8 2019.
  → https://comptroller.texas.gov/purchasing/docs/anti-bds.xlsx
  → https://comptroller.texas.gov/purchasing/publications/divestment.php
- **This is the fact the arch was missing.** Not "school money buys Israeli bonds" (unsupported)
  but: *the endowment that exists to fund Texas public schools is required by law to screen its
  investments against a state blacklist of companies that boycott Israel.* Statutory, verbatim,
  unambiguous.

**Corrections logged (these traps are easy to fall into):**
- HB 89 created Ch. **2270**, renumbered to Ch. **2271** in 2019 (HB 4170). Today's Ch. 2270 is
  the *Sudan/Iran* chapter. Citing "Chapter 2270" for Israel is now wrong.
- **SB 13 (2021) is the FOSSIL-FUEL boycott law and contains the word "Israel" zero times.** It
  is a structural clone of Ch. 808, which is why it gets confused for an anti-BDS amendment. The
  Israel statutes were last substantively amended in **2019**, not 2021.
- **HB 793 (2019)** narrowed the contractor rule to companies with **10+ employees** and
  **$100K+** contracts, and exempted **sole proprietorships** — which is exactly why Amawi (R2)
  wouldn't be covered today. Note the asymmetry: that carve-out applies to *contracts* (2271),
  **not** to *investment* (808).

### R2 — the certification reached into a school district (KEYSTONE)
- **HB 89 (2017)**, signed by Abbott, required state contractors to certify they do not and
  will not boycott Israel.
- **Bahia Amawi**, an elementary-school **speech pathologist** contracting with **Pflugerville
  ISD**, was handed the certification at the start of the 2018–19 year. She refused to sign
  and lost the work. She had been serving Arabic-speaking children with speech disorders.
- **April 25, 2019:** Judge **Robert Pitman** (W.D. Tex.) granted a preliminary injunction, in
  a **56-page opinion**, holding the law unconstitutional — it threatened to suppress
  unpopular ideas and manipulate public debate. The AG appealed.
  → https://theintercept.com/2019/04/26/in-case-brought-by-school-speech-pathologist-texas-federal-court-becomes-the-third-to-strike-down-pro-israel-oath-as-unconstitutional/
  → https://www.texastribune.org/2019/04/26/Texas-bahia-amawi-speaks-out-against-anti-israel-boycott/
- **Nuance to keep:** Texas then narrowed the law (2019) to larger contractors, which mooted
  the appeal. A sole practitioner like Amawi would not be covered today. The 2018 facts stand;
  the caption says so.

  → https://tea.texas.gov/about-tea/news-and-multimedia/news-releases/news-2023/texas-education-agency-names-new-houston-isd-superintendent-and-appoints-board-of-managers
- State control has since been **extended to June 1, 2027**.
  → https://tea.texas.gov/about-tea/news-and-multimedia/news-releases/news-2025/tea-announces-extension-of-houston-isd-intervention-and-new-appointments-to-houston-isd-board-of-managers

  (chronic low ratings at Wheatley High + board dysfunction) is an **attributed claim**, not a
  fact the arch adopts. So is the unions' counter-framing.

**Four of the eight were taken in 2026 — this year.** Fort Worth, the state's fifth-largest
district, went in March. The page is demonstrably current (it carries the May 2026 entries), so
this is not a stale artifact. The mechanism: TEC §39A.111 — five consecutive unacceptable
campus ratings forces the Commissioner to either close the campus or appoint a board. Connally's
own takeover letter, verbatim: *"two of the Connally Independent School District … campuses
earned a fifth consecutive unacceptable academic accountability rating … which requires me to
either order closure of the campuses or appoint a board of managers."*

**Editorially this changes the panel.** THE HAND currently reads as one district's story. It
isn't. Consider the type: *"Eight districts. Four this year."* Voters elected boards in eight
Texas communities; the state runs them.

**Caveat that must travel with it:** a board of managers is the top rung. A **conservator**
(directs the board but leaves it elected) and a **monitor** (recommends only) are lesser rungs —
and **TEA publishes no list of conservator or monitor districts at all**, so the true count of
districts under some form of state control is larger than eight and is **not publicly knowable**.
Don't say "eight districts are under TEA intervention"; say eight have had their elected boards
replaced.

## The state's counter-claim — attributed, not adopted

The arch does not pretend this doesn't exist. Abbott's side says it funded schools at record
levels, and the honest version is that they did put real money in — while the per-student
number that drives district budgets barely moved.

- **HB 2 (89R), signed 2025.** LBB fiscal note, verbatim: *"a negative impact of
  **($8,381,506,751)** through the biennium ending August 31, 2027."* Passed 142-5 (House),
  31-0 (Senate). Note the fiscal note also says *"The bill would make no appropriation"* — HB 2
  authorizes, it doesn't appropriate.
  → https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/fiscalnotes/pdf/HB00002F.pdf
- **Use $8.38B, not "$8.5 billion."** The $8.5B headline appears **nowhere** in the LBB note. It
  is Rep. Brad Buckley's figure — *"HB 2 delivers an additional $8.5 billion to the area that
  matters the most – the classroom."* Abbott's own release itemizes buckets that sum to
  **$8.365B**, $135M short of his own headline.
- **The basic allotment moved $55.** Statute keeps **$6,160** — unchanged since HB 3 in **2019** —
  and adds a "guaranteed yield increment adjustment." Sec. 48.2561(b), verbatim: *"the amount of
  the guaranteed yield increment adjustment... is **$55**. **This subsection expires September 1,
  2027.**"* A two-year patch that sunsets. The House had passed **$6,500 + $55**; the Senate
  stripped it back to $6,160.
- **Raise Your Hand Texas** is the only group tracking the final $55, verbatim: *"school districts
  are essentially operating with **$1,076.55 less** in a basic allotment today than they were in
  May 2019."*
  → https://www.raiseyourhandtexas.org/policy/school-funding/school-finance/
- **The ratio — the best single number here.** RYHT, verbatim, in their own session release:
  *"This session, Texas public schools needed an infusion of **$19.6 billion** in state funding to
  maintain the same purchasing power as in 2019."* Against HB 2's $8.5B headline, that's **~43%**.
  Keep their framing honest, though: the same release calls the session *"historic for public
  education"* and credits *"the largest-ever permanent pay raise."* They are not an opponent.
  → https://www.raiseyourhandtexas.org/89th-legislative-session-proves-historic-for-public-education-but-more-work-needed/
- **The out-year shape, from the LBB's own tables.** HB 2's **$55 expires 9/1/2027**. So does
  SB 2's **$1B voucher cap** — and SB 2's projected cost then triples: FY27 $989M → **FY28
  $3.07B** → **FY30 $3.98B** ($4.79B all funds). The voucher grows into the space the school
  money's patch vacates, on the same date. That symmetry is in the fiscal notes, not an argument.
- **Every Texan** (Chandra Kring Villanueva, testimony 2025-03-06), verbatim: *"If adjusted for
  inflation, the basic allotment would be **$7,546** today or **$1,386** more per student."*
  Caveat worth keeping: that testimony is titled *"in Support of HB 2"* — qualified support, not
  opposition, and pinned to the earlier $220 version. Texas AFT likewise **urged the House to
  concur**.
- **Signing date is genuinely contested:** Abbott's office ran a June 4 2025 ceremony at Salado
  Middle School; the official House Journal records **"Signed by the Governor 06/20/2025."** Both
  are documented. Report both.

**Misattributions to avoid** (all four fail at the primary source): the "$1,340/student · $18B"
line is **Kelly Rasti of the Texas Association of School Boards**, a lobbyist — not Hinojosa;
Hinojosa's *"everything that is wrong with politics"* was about **SB 2 (vouchers)**, not HB 2;
**"$7,500" is Northwest ISD's** position, not Raise Your Hand Texas's; **"$7,325" attributed to
Texas AFT is unsupported** (URL dead, no archive). No verified source puts the inflation-adjusted
allotment at $8,000+.
